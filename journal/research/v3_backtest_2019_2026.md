# Does the v3 desk make money over time? 6 of 8 years yes — 2022 says the tail is the risk.

Date: 2026-07-23. SHADOW research note. Nothing here promotes anything, changes the live
desk, or softens a threshold. Artifacts:
`journal/scorecards/options_etf_gate_v3.json` (+ `_trades.jsonl`, `_equity.csv`),
pre-registration `journal/experiments/options_etf_gate_v3.json` (written and committed
before any results). Code: `desk/etf_backtest.py::run_v3`. Commit `e2c16d1`.

## What was tested

The live v3 ETF rule, replayed on data we already have (2019-07-18 → 2026-07-23):

- **Universe / signal:** 31 frozen liquid ETFs, structure routed by the **point-in-time
  Qlib lean** (walk-forward export `qlib_lab/journal/exports/qlib_score_history.parquet`,
  no lookahead): +1 → put credit spread, −1 → call credit spread, 0 → iron condor.
- **Veto:** eruption-only (magnitude/calm are advisory in v3, so they do not filter here).
- **Filters:** IV/RV richness, **tastytrade POP-at-expiry ≥ 0.51**, $1000 per-trade cap.
- **Exit:** **50% profit target** — close the first day the position can be bought back for
  ≤ 50% of the entry credit — detected by BS-repricing each leg along the daily underlying
  path; fall back to expiry intrinsic if never hit.
- **Inference:** one observation per non-overlapping entry; **same-date entries clustered**
  into one observation (correlated names must not fake independence). Canonical gate on the
  cluster-adjusted series.

## Headline results (cluster-adjusted, 302 independent observations)

| year | trades | win rate | PnL | PF | % exited at target |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 58 | 84% | +$696 | 1.45 | 83% |
| 2020 | 155 | 85% | +$3,597 | 1.96 | 84% |
| 2021 | 183 | 85% | +$5,266 | 2.63 | 84% |
| **2022** | 180 | **61%** | **−$7,025** | **0.48** | 60% |
| 2023 | 222 | 74% | −$1,273 | 0.87 | 73% |
| 2024 | 196 | 81% | +$2,976 | 1.54 | 79% |
| 2025 | 171 | 87% | +$3,254 | 1.77 | 84% |
| 2026 | 75 | 88% | +$2,223 | 2.47 | 88% |

**Totals:** profitable **6 of 8 years**, cluster-adjusted **+$2,830**, PF **1.35**, ~80%
win. Structures traded: iron condor 765, call spread 220, put spread 255 (neutral lean is
the most common, so condors dominate). Exits: 965 at target, 275 at expiry.

## Finding 1 — the risk is the tail, not the average

2022 alone lost **−$7,025** at a 61% win rate (PF 0.48). Short-premium selling gets run
over in a sustained selloff: many small wins, then a cluster of near-max losses when the
whole cross-section trends down together. The average year is healthy; the strategy's real
exposure is a bear-market tail. **Managing that tail — not raising the win rate — is the
thing to fix.**

## Finding 2 — the 50% profit target made LESS than holding to expiry

Same entries, settled at true expiry instead of the 50% target:

| exit policy | cluster-adjusted total |
|---|---:|
| 50% profit target | **+$2,830** |
| hold to expiry | **+$5,210** |

The target caps each winner at half the credit while losers still run toward max loss, so
raw dollars fall. What the target buys is a **higher win rate and faster capital turnover**
(965 of 1240 trades closed early, ~30 days held), not more money. The common "always manage
at 50%" instinct did not increase return on this sample. Whether the turnover/psychology is
worth ~45% less total P&L is a judgment call — the data does not say the target is free.

## Finding 3 — POP ≥ 51% is doing real work

A 30-delta iron condor has ~40% probability of profit *at expiry* and is correctly dropped
by the 0.51 floor **unless** the premium is rich enough to widen the profit zone past
breakeven. So condors only trade when they are genuinely paid to; the filter is not
cosmetic. Put/call spreads (~30-delta short, POP ~65-70%) pass routinely.

## The gate still fails — and why it doesn't surprise us

Cluster-adjusted **DSR −37.2** (PBO 0.123 passes; DSR fails), so v3 does **not** clear the
canonical gate and stays SHADOW. This is the same structural issue documented in
`qlib_filter_and_dsr_bar.md`: the Deflated Sharpe compares a **per-trade** Sharpe (~0.3-0.5
for premium selling) against an `e_max` that rises with `n_trials` (93 here → bar ~2.5).
A premium-selling payoff cannot reach that per-trade Sharpe regardless of quality. PF 1.35
and six green years do not override it. The gate is a promotion test, not a profitability
test; the two are answering different questions here.

## Declared limitations (this is an UPPER BOUND)

- **Constant entry IV along the repricing path.** No historical IV surface exists, so the
  50%-target crossing is detected with entry IV held fixed. This ignores vol expansion —
  which for a short book is *optimistic* (a real vol spike raises the cost to close and
  delays/denies the target). 2022 in particular is likely **understated**.
- **Model (BS) entry credit, not natural bid/ask.** Optimistic vs live fills.
- **Proxied IV at entry** (RV20 × market premium factor), a market-wide scalar.

Real-money results would be worse than the above, not better.

## What to do next (NOT by retuning v3)

Per the pre-registration's no-p-hacking rule: any change is a **new pre-registered v4**,
re-counting trials — we do not tweak v3 on this same data until it looks better.

1. **Attack the 2022 tail** (highest value): a trend/regime filter (don't sell premium into
   a sustained cross-sectional downtrend) or a cheap long-tail hedge. Register as v4 and
   re-run this exact harness.
2. **Re-examine the 50% target** vs hold-to-expiry, and vs a loss-side stop (the target is
   profit-only today; a stop would attack the tail directly).
3. Drill into the 2022 trade log (`_trades.jsonl`) to see whether the loss was broad or a
   few underlyings, which informs whether a regime filter or per-name control is the fix.
